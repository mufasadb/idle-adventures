## Named Principles and Frameworks

*   **Gestalt Law of Similarity:** Visual consistency (like the color coding of Fortnite's Mist Monsters) allows players to instantly categorize enemy types and threat levels.
*   **Gestalt Law of Proximity:** Placing enemies close together (as seen in Serious Sam) helps players perceive them as a single encounter group rather than isolated targets, reducing cognitive load.
*   **Gestalt Law of Closure:** Broken environmental structures like arches naturally prompt the player's brain to complete the shape, drawing their eye through the space.
*   **Gestalt Law of Symmetry:** Symmetrical level design provides visual balance and helps center the player's focus on main pathways.
*   **Gestalt Law of Continuity:** Flowing elements like ramps or stairs smoothly guide the player's eye toward objectives without explicit signage.
*   **Gestalt Law of Common Fate:** Architectural lines that converge or point in the same direction guide player movement down intended paths.
*   **Negative Space:** Empty space in an environment is used to reduce visual noise and highlight important areas, objectives, or safe landing zones.
*   **Rule of Thirds / Golden Ratio:** Traditional 2D composition rules that can guide framing in games, though they are less reliable when the player controls the 3D camera.
*   **Affordances (Primitive Shapes Language):** Primitive shapes inherently communicate function—round implies safe, rectangular implies solid/useful cover, and pointy implies danger.
*   **Cognitive Load:** Grouping enemies or simplifying complex UI elements reduces the mental effort required, allowing players to focus on core gameplay.
*   **Flow / Rhythm:** Structuring encounters in clear, repeatable patterns helps players learn, master mechanics, and fall into a comfortable state of flow.
*   **User Centered Design:** The philosophy that all game systems (even math-heavy progression or combat systems) must be built around how the player feels and experiences them over time.
